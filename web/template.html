CDOT route 034A · MP 61 to MP 92

US 34, Estes Park to Loveland

Thirty-one miles from downtown Estes Park, down Big Thompson Canyon, to US 287 in Loveland. Where people get hurt, why, and what would prevent it.

Crash records 2019–2025 (the seven full years since the canyon was rebuilt) unless noted. Scroll to follow the map.

THE CORRIDOR

Four very different roads in one route

The corridor drops about 750 m (2,500 ft) from Estes Park to Loveland. Town streets at the top, an 18.5-mile two-lane canyon, a short rural stretch past the canyon mouth, then a four-lane urban arterial.

WHERE CRASHES HAPPEN

Most crashes are in Loveland. The worst ones are in the canyon.

Loveland's crashes are mostly low-speed rear-end and turning crashes. Mile for mile the canyon has fewer crashes, but nearly half of them injure someone.

BIG THOMPSON CANYON · MP 65–MP 83.5

Curves and run-off-road crashes

  • 75% of canyon crashes happen on a curve, and 72% involve a single vehicle.
  • 25 of the canyon's 41 fatal or serious crashes hit a fixed object or rolled over.
  • The densest cluster of severe crashes is MP 70–MP 72, with 11.
  • The severe-crash rate is 14.6 per 100 million vehicle-miles, 1.7 times Loveland's.
CURVE BY CURVE · MP 65–MP 88

The tightest curves crash most, and a few stand out

A curve inventory from CDOT's centerline finds 89 curves in the canyon, 4.8 per mile. Curves under 250 ft averaged 6.8 crashes each, more than three times the gentler ones. Curves that bend to the driver's left had twice the severe crashes of right-hand curves (21 against 11), mostly vehicles leaving the outside edge.

MOTORCYCLES

5% of crashes, 33% of the severe ones

Motorcycles were involved in 24 of the 72 fatal or serious crashes. In the canyon, 12 of the 29 motorcycle crashes were fatal or serious. Most happen April to September.

WHEN

Winter and night carry extra risk

Summer has the most crashes because it has the most traffic. Per mile driven, November to February is about 55% riskier than June to September.

DRIVER FACTORS

Impairment is a canyon problem at night

Alcohol or drugs were suspected in 7% of crashes but 19% of fatal or serious ones. 47 of the 103 impaired-driving crashes were in the canyon, and 39% happened between 9 pm and 5 am.

Phone use is recorded in only 2 crashes, almost certainly an undercount.

WILDLIFE · MP 84–MP 90

100 wildlife crashes, none fatal or serious

Deer (48) and elk (43) are about even. 73 of the 100 are between the canyon mouth and west Loveland, where CPW maps elk winter range and highway crossings. November is the peak month, and 57 happened on dark, unlighted road.

SUN GLARE · 2007–2025

Low sun ahead adds crashes where the road runs open and straight

Eastbound drivers with the morning sun ahead, and westbound drivers with the evening sun ahead, crash more often than their share of traffic predicts in Loveland and just past the canyon mouth. In the canyon, walls block the low sun.

LIGHTING

happen at night on an unlit road

Lighting is from the officer's crash report. Street lights show up only in Loveland from MP 88.5 east and in downtown Estes Park; the road between is dark at night.

GOAL

Halve fatal and serious crashes

The proposed corridor goal is 5 or fewer fatal or serious crashes a year, down from about 10, with zero as the long-term aim under Colorado's Strategic Highway Safety Plan. A road safety audit of the canyon is the next step to pick the first curves to treat.
